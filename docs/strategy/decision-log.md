# Decision Log

## 2026-08-02 — Asia pivot and the vintage trade

Status: Decided. Supersedes the open questions in `expansion-research-brief.md`.

### What the research settled

Three deep-research reports were run against the four expansion ideas. They started
from different questions and landed on the same structure.

The core asset is one thing: the ability to translate Japan-side context into another
language. Four ideas turned out to be one business with three points of sale.

- **Primary revenue.** Japan-side intelligence sold to J1 and J2 clubs for the
  partner-country and Asian quota slots — the inbound direction, not the European
  outbound one. Weighted score 4.40 of 5, ahead of every other segment. Clubs already
  hold budget lines of JPY 1.5–5M for scouting tools and JPY 3–10M for external
  commissioned research, so the work competes for an existing line rather than
  creating a new one.
- **Second revenue.** ASEAN top-tier clubs buying Japanese player intelligence.
  Score 3.75. Owner-level decisions, so the sales cycle is fast, but only the top
  ten or so clubs have real budget.
- **Fast cash, low ceiling.** Vintage Japanese football apparel sold overseas.
  First revenue in about two weeks, ceiling around JPY 300k per month for one person.
- **Not a revenue line.** English-language J.League media. It loses money on its own
  at any realistic scale. Its job is different, and it is the load-bearing piece.

### Why the media sits in the middle

The strongest objection in the first report: agents hand clubs the same contextual
information for free, because they are trying to sell a player. That kills any
independent information service unless it can prove it is not an agent.

Published English analysis is the only proof that works. A sales deck asserts
neutrality; two years of public writing demonstrates it. So the media is not a
customer-acquisition channel first — it is the evidence of no conflict of interest,
which is what makes the B2B work sellable at all.

### The vintage rule

One line governs the whole trade: never pay more than JPY 10,000 for a single item.

It is the margin boundary. International shipping is near-fixed at JPY 3,000–4,000
per garment, so at a realistic overseas price of USD 150 the buy price has to stay
under JPY 10,000 for the gross to clear JPY 6,000.

It is also the legal boundary. The second-hand dealer identity-verification duty is
waived below JPY 10,000, and clothing is not among the excluded categories. Above
that line the duty applies and cannot be satisfied through anonymous flea-market
shipping. Sourcing above JPY 10,000 moves to auction sites, vintage shops, and
dealer markets. The licence itself is required regardless of amount.

Sell vintage track tops on Grailed and Depop, not eBay. They trade as fashion, not
as football memorabilia, and the buyers are in a different place.

### Ruled out, with reasons

- New replica kits, no-inventory. 17 percent gross, negative once shipping is in the
  price, competing against USD 15–25 counterfeits.
- Listing on Mercari. Prohibited outright. Fine as a sourcing channel.
- Thai and Indonesian media. Local RPM of USD 1.33–2.38 against USD 180–825 of
  translation cost per article.
- Mid and lower ASEAN clubs. Score 2.65, no budget exists.
- J.League match footage in any form. Rights sit with the league and clips are
  prohibited. Text, original graphics, and own photography only.
- Match shirts in S and M. JASPO L is roughly a European M; overseas buyers cannot
  wear them.

### First real price observation, 2026-08-02

A Mizuno Yomiuri Nippon FC Verdy track jacket, deadstock with the J.League hang tag
still attached, listed domestically at JPY 18,000. The comparable Tokyo Verdy 1994-95
Mizuno track jacket sold overseas at USD 272, and a 1993-95 away shirt in new-in-box
condition at USD 285.99, against USD 105 for the same club's worn shirt.

That is roughly a 2.3x spread, and it answers the open question in the research brief:
the arbitrage gap has not been closed by the specialist buyers. Two premiums stack and
both are real — track jackets over match shirts, because they sell into vintage fashion
rather than football memorabilia, and deadstock over worn.

Authenticity on that piece is not in doubt. The Mizuno product code format, the colour
named 読売日本SCモデル, the JAN prefix, the JASPO body-measurement notation, the BRABER
fabric hang tag, and Japanese manufacture all match the period. More to the point, no
counterfeit market exists for a 1993 domestic club garment that moves a few units a
year — forgery follows volume, currency, and global recognition, and this has none.

It was still passed on, because JPY 18,000 crosses the verification threshold and the
duty cannot be satisfied through anonymous flea-market shipping. The correct response
is to split sourcing by price: flea-market apps below JPY 10,000, auction sites and
physical dealers above it. Auction sites disclose the counterparty, and the better
pieces surface there anyway.

### The spread that matters is the channel, not the sourcing

Second observation, same day. A 1998 Japan home shirt, asics, Nakata #8, JASPO L,
near-mint, listed domestically at JPY 14,100 including shipping.

The comparables split by where the shirt is sold, not by what it is:

- Grailed, peer to peer, graded 9/10 — sold at USD 165
- Classic Football Shirts, graded 8/10 — USD 629.99, out of stock
- Classic Football Shirts, graded 10/10 — USD 699.99, out of stock

The same shirt carries a 4x range depending on the seller, and both specialist
listings cleared at the top of it. The sourcing spread from JPY 14,100 to USD 165 is
real but it is the smaller of the two. The larger one sits between being a peer seller
and being a seller whose authentication, grading, and written context a collector
trusts.

That reframes the trade. Volume is the wrong lever. The return is higher in raising
realised price per item from USD 165 toward USD 350 than in moving more units, and the
thing that raises it is exactly the core asset — explaining, in English, why the
tone-on-tone flame jacquard is the mark reproductions miss, why 1998 was the first
appearance, why the number 8 carries what it carries.

The vintage trade and the intelligence work turn out to run on the same skill. That is
a stronger link between the two revenue lines than the plan originally assumed.

Practical consequence: revise the Day 31-90 target away from unit count toward realised
price per item, and treat the written listing as the deliverable being tested.

### Caveat carried forward

Every price observation was taken immediately after the 2026 World Cup and carries a
tournament premium that will decay. Re-check the comparisons before scaling stock.

### What this does not change

`positioning.md` stays as it is. The European-facing position remains the current
outward source-of-truth until a J.League club has actually paid for a report. Desk
research plus one artifact is not evidence of a market.
